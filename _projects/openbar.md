Can findings of more efficient attentional control in bilinguals be replicated, how well, and can we do better? The OPEN Bilingual Advantage Replicability project (OPEN BAR) aims to answer these questions. 

OPEN BAR is funded by a Fondo Italiano per la Scienza (FIS) 3 Starting Grant to Giacomo Spinelli. Originally proposed with Sapienza University of Rome as a Host Institution, it has later been hosted by the University of Rome Tor Vergata. The abstract of the original proposal can be found below. A copy of the complete proposal (excluding the more technical parts) can be found <a href="{{ '/assets/pdf/openbar-proposal.pdf' | relative_url }}" target="_blank" rel="noopener">here</a>.

## Abstract

There is undeniable appeal in the notion that speaking a second language would not only confer social, cultural, and economic advantages, but also benefit the performance of such cognitive functions as attentional control. However, following initial enthusiasm with this **bilingual advantage (BA) hypothesis**, evidence for such an advantage has been **hard to replicate consistently**. These difficulties, however, have never been fatal for the hypothesis, because they either came from replications that are not of the direct type (i.e., faithful reproductions of the original study to the extent possible) and/or they involved measures affected by several problems (e.g., poor psychometric properties) that could explain why null results may arise. The result is a stalemate where little progress is made despite the **many resources** researchers and funders continue to invest in the topic. By directly addressing the replicability of BA studies in an innovative combination of open, meta-, and cognitive science, **OPEN BAR promises to break this stalemate**. 

Specifically, OPEN BAR will: 
1. **enable the replicability** of BA studies by developing a community-augmented web app specifically designed to support the direct replication of such studies, also through tools for sample planning and meta-analysis;
2. **estimate their replicability** by conducting high-powered, registered direct replication for a selection of studies, including both behavioral and neural measures;
3. **enhance their replicability** by developing a new type of study with a theoretically grounded design and an unprecedented wealth of observations which will address all of the obstacles to observing correlations between bilingualism and behavioral/neural measures in BA studies. 

By helping clarify the existence and nature of potential BAs, OPEN BAR will have a tangible impact on the approach to bilingualism in policy making. With a better sense of the replicability of BA studies, a hub to exchange information and resources rapidly, and new terrain for transparent, collaborative initiatives to take place, **BA research itself will be also deeply affected, as will be the image of it in the public eye**. Finally, by adopting and extending cutting-edge open science practices, OPEN BAR will make a **lasting contribution to the scientific community at large**.
